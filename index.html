About me

Research is only as durable as the infrastructure around it.

I am an associate professor and department head of the Open Publishing Exchange at Kansas State University Libraries. My work focuses on scholarly communication, research cyberinfrastructure, FAIR and AI-ready data, persistent identifiers, computational reproducibility, data publishing, and cyberbiosecurity.

I lead efforts in data curation, publishing, and open access while working with researchers to make scholarly outputs more accessible, trustworthy, and useful over time. I also contribute to agricultural and biodiversity informatics through AgBioData, i5k, and related national research-data initiatives.

My training is in computational biology and bioinformatics. Before joining K-State, I worked as a computational biologist with the USDA Agricultural Research Service and as a scientific informatics developer at Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory. That path still shapes how I approach scholarly infrastructure: as something that must work for both people and machines.

How I approach the work

Open is an operating model, not a final checkbox.

Good infrastructure has to preserve context, distribute credit, support verification, and remain usable after a project team moves on.

That means treating metadata, documentation, governance, licensing, accessibility, and long-term stewardship as part of the research itself. I am especially interested in the places where a technical system succeeds only because a community can understand and sustain it.
