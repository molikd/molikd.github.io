The Molik Lab

Infrastructure is part of the scientific method.

We study and build the connective tissue of research: the data systems, workflows, identifiers, standards, and publishing practices that let knowledge travel.

Who we are

A networked lab for networked problems.

The Molik Lab is a collaborative research group at Kansas State University working across scholarly communication, research computing, data stewardship, and the life sciences.

We are intentionally organized around projects and partnerships rather than disciplinary walls. A repository problem may also be a metadata problem, a workflow problem, a governance problem, and a training problem. We bring those pieces together.

The lab is led by David C. Molik, associate professor and department head of the Open Publishing Exchange at K-State Libraries. Its work draws on collaborations with researchers, students, librarians, software engineers, publishers, research-computing teams, and national data communities.

LAB

Our central question

How can research infrastructure preserve enough context to make scientific work findable, trustworthy, reproducible, and reusable—by people and by machines?

  • Open by default
  • Machine-actionable
  • Community-governed
  • Built to last
Research directions

From research objects to research ecosystems.

Our work focuses on four connected areas where better infrastructure can change what research communities are able to do.

01

Reproducible research objects

Packaging workflows, software environments, containers, notebooks, data, provenance, and publications so computational research can be inspected and rerun.

02

FAIR and AI-ready data

Designing metadata, identifiers, relationships, and curation practices that support responsible reuse by both researchers and computational systems.

03

Agricultural and biodiversity informatics

Improving discovery, standards, and long-term stewardship for genomic, genetic, breeding, and biodiversity data through community infrastructure.

04

Cyberbiosecurity and research integrity

Understanding how data provenance, access, system design, and publication practices affect trust, security, and resilience in the life sciences.

How we work

Build, document, teach, hand off.

The goal is not merely to make a prototype. It is to leave behind infrastructure and practice that a community can understand, govern, and sustain.

Start with the workflow

We observe how research actually moves—from collection and computation through review, publication, preservation, and reuse.

Design for relationships

We connect people, data, software, instruments, grants, publications, and institutions through meaningful metadata and identifiers.

Plan for transfer

Documentation, training, governance, accessibility, and maintenance are treated as research deliverables—not cleanup work.

People and partnerships

The roster follows the question.

The lab currently operates through a distributed network of collaborators rather than a fixed list of permanent members.

Projects may bring together participants from K-State Libraries, research computing, computer science, agriculture, biosecurity, publishers, repositories, and national scientific-data communities. This structure reflects the work: infrastructure problems rarely belong to only one department.

I welcome conversations with undergraduate and graduate researchers, faculty, librarians, developers, community infrastructure projects, and external partners.
